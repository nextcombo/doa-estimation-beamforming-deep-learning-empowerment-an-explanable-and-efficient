III. Introduction
Direction of Arrival (DOA) estimation, also known as ranging, is a core technology in sensor array signal processing and is widely applied in multiple engineering fields such as radar, radio astronomy, sonar, navigation, remote sensing, wireless communication, biomedical engineering, and speech processing. Despite extensive research proposing various algorithms and conducting in-depth performance analysis, most studies have focused on the asymptotic scenarios involving multiple frames. However, in practical dynamic application scenarios like automotive radars, the available DOA estimation data often only includes a limited number of radar sensor array frames, and in the most challenging cases, it may even be just a single frame.
The study of DOA estimation methods has undergone a long journey. The traditional (Bartlett) beamformer, born during World War II, was based on the Fourier spectrum analysis of spatial-temporal sampling data, but it faced issues with high sidelobe levels and limited Rayleigh resolution. Subsequently, the minimum power undistorted response (MPDR) beamformer and the minimum variance undistorted response (MVDR) beamformer (commonly referred to as Capon beamforming), were proposed, aimed at improving source estimation performance in close-range source scenarios.
In addition to beamforming methods, parameter subspace methods (including Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC), Rotating Invariant Technique Parameter Estimation (ESPRIT) and its variants) achieve DOA estimation through the second order statistics of the data; the nonlinear least squares (NLS) method (also known as deterministic maximum likelihood (DML) estimation) requires multidimensional grid search for solution, but its computational complexity grows exponentially with the number of sources. To alleviate computational burden, convex approximation methods based on sparse regularization (such as Compressed Sensing) and iterative adaptive methods (IAA) have been proposed, but the former has strict requirements on array design, and the latter still faces computational cost issues.
These traditional methods have significant limitations: requiring known source number, reliance on multiple snapshot data, and not suitable for sparse arrays or complex computations. In recent years, although data-driven deep learning DOA estimation methods offer advantages such as fast inference speed and strong super-resolution capabilities, they generally lack interpretability; existing model-based deep learning methods are still limited in performance under unknown array structure and source number scenarios. Therefore, exploring interpretable, generalizable, and high-performance deep network architectures remains a key challenge in the field of signal processing.
This paper proposes a deep learning network, deep-MPDR, that is explainable and efficient. It maps the principles of MPDR beamforming to a deep learning framework, integrating domain knowledge from traditional models with the advantages of deep learning. Through multi-scene experiments, this method outperforms traditional algorithms and other deep learning methods in terms of inference time, accuracy, generalization, and interpretability, providing a new solution for single-snapshot DOA estimation.

By converting the MPDR beamformer into a deep learning framework, this network avoids high computational costs associated with large matrix inversion, achieving rapid inference; meanwhile, thanks to model-driven design, its parameter count is significantly less than traditional data-driven deep learning methods, enhancing generalizability and effectively adapting to complex scenarios with unknown source counts or sparse arrays.

Through spectral analysis of learnable parameters, the physical correlation with MPDR beamformers is verified, addressing the "black box" problem in deep learning.
